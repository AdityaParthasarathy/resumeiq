How it reads your resume

Four passes over the same document.

Every upload goes through four independent, inspectable passes — no single opaque score. Scroll and watch the same resume get annotated by each one.

{{ macros.resume_mock(id="story-resume") }}

01

Structure & completeness

Section detection scans for short, header-like lines to find your Skills, Education, Experience, and Projects — deliberately avoiding false positives from a bullet that just mentions “skills” mid-sentence. Six categories get scored out of 100: structure, skills, education, projects, contact info, and completeness.

02

Role-specific ATS keywords

Your resume is checked against a keyword bank for the role you pick, in three passes: exact match, then a synonym map (“JS” → “JavaScript”), then fuzzy typo correction (“dockr” → “Docker”). Missing must-have keywords surface first.

03

Bullet-by-bullet impact

Every bullet is parsed with spaCy: is the opening verb strong or weak? Is there a quantified result? Is it written in passive voice? Weak bullets get context-aware verb suggestions — not just a red flag.

04

Prioritized, human-readable feedback

All three passes feed a rule-based template engine that ranks suggestions from critical to low priority, in plain language — so you know what to fix first.

Why rule-based

No black box. Every score is a rule you can read.

Most resume tools either give vague advice or hide behind a model that can't explain itself. ResumeIQ has neither problem — there's no model making the decisions.
